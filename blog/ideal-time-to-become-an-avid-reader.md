title: "Given that reading can harm pronunciation, when is the ideal time to become an avid reader?"

I find that for people who want a native-like accent,
it is best to start reading as late as possible.
Depending on when you start reading,
you are basically trading faster progress and literacy for better pronunciation.
If you want a good accent,
I recommend starting to read novels after about a year.
Waiting longer will probably give diminishing returns,
but go for it if you do not like reading
or if you are really aiming for perfect pronunciation.

Some people don't really care much about pronunciation,
and in that case they might focus on reading right from the beginning.
Of course,
reading novels will be difficult for beginners
unless they do a lot of immersion before they start reading.

And of course,
you can always improve your pronunciation later.
To do that,
you'll have to go through a period where
you spend more time listening and less time reading.
Additionally, you need to deliberately practice pronunciation through **imitation exercises**.
Pick a line from a recording of a native speaker and listen closely to how they say it.
Then record yourself saying the same line, trying to match the sounds, rhythm, and pitch accent.
Play the two recordings back to back and listen for differences.
