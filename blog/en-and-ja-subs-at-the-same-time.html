I immerse with English and Japanese subs on at the same time. Is this a bad idea?

April 24, 2023 (updated November 24, 2026) — Tatsumoto Ren

If you watch with both English and Japanese subtitle tracks on screen, it is better to keep them hidden most of the time. In mpv, you can press v to show or hide primary subtitles. When you feel like you didn't understand something, you can turn them on temporarily and check what you heard. Mpvacious has key bindings for toggling secondary subtitles. By default, you can also reveal the secondary subtitles by hovering over them with your mouse.

How you use subtitles depends on what you're trying to improve.

Training your listening ability

Two sets of subtitles take up a lot of screen space and are hard to ignore. If you leave them on, you'll probably read them without even realizing it. It may happen unconsciously, automatically, accidentally. Even a few words caught out of the corner of your eye can give away what someone said and disrupt your listening practice. If you really want to improve your listening ability, you should hide all subtitles. That is the only way to make sure you are not cheating.

Improving your overall comprehension

To learn effectively, it is important not to read the English subtitles. If you do, and if you don't pay attention to the Japanese subtitles, you will not make any progress. If you jump straight to English every time, you'll get more practice reading English than understanding Japanese. Try to understand the Japanese subtitles first.

If you must use English subtitles, try to make them less accessible. Require a key press or a mouse movement to reveal them. That gives you a chance to try the Japanese first.

Then let's think about whether English subtitles are actually helping, or whether it would be better to get rid of them completely. As I mentioned in Should I put English translations on Bilingual TSCs, relying on translations does not help with language acquisition. They might give you a rough idea of what a sentence means, but they do not help you learn how ideas are expressed in Japanese or how the grammar works. Focus on understanding the Japanese subtitles first. Use English subtitles only if you are completely lost and don't understand the whole sentence.

The worst part about reading English subtitles is that you can feel like you understand the original text when you really only understand the translation. On top of that, using English subtitles can create false associations between English words and Japanese words with similar meanings. That can bias your understanding of Japanese.

Conclusion

It is OK to keep Japanese subtitles visible, but English subtitles are better left hidden. Hiding both English and Japanese subtitles is best if you want to improve your listening. And if you do not want to keep toggling subtitles on and off, consider watching some shows completely raw and others with Japanese subs always on.
