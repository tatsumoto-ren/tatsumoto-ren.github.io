Understanding Basic Japanese Grammar

9 августа 2026 — Тацумото Рен

Примечание: Обратите внимание, что эта статья ещё не переведена полностью. Если вы хотите помочь, присоединяйтесь к нашему сообществу и создайте pull request на GitHub.

Understanding Basic Japanese Grammar by Nishiguchi Koichi is a grammar book.

  • ISBN-10: 475740168X.
  • ISBN-13: 978-4757401686.
  • Download from Ajatt-Tools channel.
  • The PDF can also be found on annas-archive and Z-library. Links change often, so search by ISBN.

You probably already know what AJATT thinks about textbooks and grammar study. Textbooks are the devil's spawn. Studying grammar is a confusing waste of time when it comes to actually teaching you how to use the language. So why am I recommending a book that has "grammar" in the title?

We recommend using grammar only as a tool to improve comprehension. Grammar guides should be written and read not to explain grammar, but to give you examples. Don't worry about the rules. When you read a grammar guide, focus on understanding example sentences. The focus should be on learning patterns and getting used to frequently spoken collocations, phrases and sentences. You need to absorb the patterns used in your target language. Being exposed to thousands of correct Japanese sentences will build your sense of Japanese. It will develop the instinct to decide, "This is the right way to say X". the instinct we all use when we speak our native language.

This book has lots of example sentences. It includes no more explanation of rules than is necessary to get the job done. In fewer than 200 pages (197), it packs just under 2,000 sentences. It starts simple and gradually gets more complex. You see each pattern repeated many times, allowing you to fully grasp it before moving on to the next one.

Pros:

  • Simple, gradual progress.
  • No boring explanations. The book cuts to the chase and provides clear explanations of exceptions.
  • Tons of example sentences.
  • Lots of repetition of concepts.
  • Kanji throughout, so you read real Japanese with furigana on every character. It acknowledges that you're a beginner and helps you without hand‑cuffing you (no romaji!).
  • Stays useful as a reference after you finish. The table of contents is very detailed.

Cons:

  • Not confusing, but it can get dull. The content is largely limited to college‑student situations, which may not be relevant to everyone.
  • We suggest skipping the boring or redundant parts. There's no need to force yourself. Vocabulary is limited to those college contexts, so you won't learn many new words. On the other hand, the narrow vocab can help you focus on grammar patterns and concepts. Only a few illustrations are included, and they're all small and in black and white.

Like almost any book, this one isn't without flaws. Overall, however, it makes a great starter. It gives you a solid grammatical foundation and sets you on the path to learning more vocabulary.
